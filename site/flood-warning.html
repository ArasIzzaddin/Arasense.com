Lafaw · flood early warning as a service

Flash floods give hours.
Lafaw gives days.

Lafaw is Arasense's operational flood early-warning platform for cities and water authorities in semi-arid, data-scarce regions. It turns a 51-member weather ensemble into a probability of flooding for every basin that threatens the city, six days ahead and down to the next six hours, then tells the people responsible in their own language. Every forecast is scored against what actually fell, and the scores are part of the service.

The problem

Dry wadis. Deadly in three hours.

In semi-arid cities the streams are dry for most of the year and the gauges, where they exist, are few. A convective storm fills a 50 km² basin and sends its peak through the suburbs in one to three hours. The December 2021 flash flood in Erbil killed people and destroyed homes in a single morning. A single deterministic forecast, read the evening before, gave no useful warning: the information was in the spread of the ensemble, and nobody was reading it per basin.

What exists today One number

A city-wide rainfall forecast, deterministic, with no link to the basins that actually flood and no record of whether it was right.

What is missing Hours of lead time

Sensors alone warn when the water is already in the channel. Useful warning has to come from the forecast, with the uncertainty stated.

What Lafaw does Probability per basin

Rain, soil wetness, runoff and peak flow for every member of the ensemble, graded against the basin's own reference flows, published with its verification.

How it works

From the ensemble to the wadi.

Map every basin that threatens the city

Catchments are delineated from the 30 m Copernicus DEM, with flow paths, concentration times, land cover from ESA WorldCover, soils from SoilGrids and a curve number per basin. Reference flows for 2-, 5- and 20-year floods come from a Gumbel fit on sixteen years of reanalysis rain.

Run the whole ensemble through each basin

All 51 ECMWF members are converted to runoff and peak flow for each basin, day by day, with antecedent wetness. The fraction of members that exceed the reference flows gives the probability of watch, warning and danger levels, up to six days ahead.

Nowcast the next six hours

Hourly rain for the coming six hours gives the burst start and the arrival of the peak at the station, so a warning says when the water comes, not only that it may.

Alert, record, verify

Bilingual alerts go to the duty officers by messaging channels. Every forecast issue is stored and scored against observed rain from the ECMWF analysis, NASA satellite rain and the client's own gauges. Scores are on a page the client can open at any time.

Verification

Scored before it is sold, honestly framed

Before the first deployment, the method was run as a hindcast on every daily forecast of the past two and a half years for the nine basins of Erbil, with three global models, and scored against the observed rain. These are the numbers, including the weak one.

0.79 Probability of detection at the watch level, one day ahead: four of five wet days with runoff were flagged the evening before.
0.41 Critical success index three days ahead. Lead time costs little skill: the signal is in the ensemble, not in the last run.
2.5 yr Hindcast length, every daily issue, nine basins, three models. A multi-model majority raised the index to 0.44.
What does not work yet. At the warning level the false-alarm ratio is high, 0.7 to 0.9, for every model and every combination we tried. It is systematic, which means it is a threshold-and-observation problem, not a forecast problem, and it is what the first season's gauges are for. We publish the number rather than tune it away.
Flood maps

Where the water goes, and how deep

The same terrain that gives the basins gives the maps. Flood corridors per reach for the 2-, 5-, 20- and 100-year floods, and a two-dimensional inundation model (the local-inertial scheme of LISFLOOD-FP and SFINCS, written by us) that routes a design storm over the 30 m grid and returns depth, extent and the outflow hydrograph at the station.

Corridors

Flood classes per reach

Height-above-nearest-drainage with a synthetic rating per channel reach, four return periods, as a map layer on the operations dashboard and as GeoTIFF for the client's GIS.

Inundation

Depth and extent per storm

Rain-on-grid two-dimensional routing with mass balance checked on every run; the culverts-open and culverts-blocked scenarios, the second being what happened in Erbil in 2021.

Validation

What we checked, what we could not

Sentinel-1 next-day imagery sees the river floodplains but not wadi floods that recede in hours. Corridors are therefore validated with flood marks, photographs and the first season's gauges, and upgraded with a drone terrain model where it matters.

The service

A monthly service, not a box

What the client receives

Operations, every day

The outlook and nowcast refreshed around the clock; alerts to named duty officers in Kurdish, Arabic, Italian or English; the operations dashboard with basins, sensors, flood layers and the forecast archive; a self-check page that reports its own health; a short monthly report with the season's verification.

What the client keeps

Their data, their sensors

Lafaw reads the water-level and rain sensors the client already owns and leaves their records with them. It runs without sensors too: the forecast side needs only the terrain and the weather ensemble, which is why it can be deployed in weeks and improved as gauges arrive.
